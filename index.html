❓ Help & Field Guide
🧮 Calculator — Main Fields
Principal
The total amount of money you're starting with — your combined savings pot at the beginning of each life phase. For the first phase (Now–55), this is all of Keeron and Erin's savings added together.
💡 When you move from one phase to the next, this number is automatically carried forward from the previous ending balance.
Interest %
The average annual return you expect your investments to earn. For example, 7% means your money grows by roughly 7% per year before withdrawals.
💡 Higher in early phases (more growth assets), lower in retirement (more conservative mix).
Inflation % & ADJ toggle
Inflation is how much prices rise each year — set to 3% here as a default. When the ADJ toggle is ON, your monthly withdrawals automatically increase each year to keep up with inflation. When OFF, withdrawals stay the same dollar amount throughout.
💡 Turning ADJ ON is more realistic — $9,000 today won't feel like $9,000 in 10 years.
Compounding
How often your interest is calculated and added to your balance. Monthly is the most common and most accurate for most investment accounts.
Duration (Years)
How many years this phase lasts. The phases are pre-set: Now–55 is 4 years, 55–60 and 60–65 are 5 years each, and retirement (65+) is 35 years.
Gross Monthly (+ or −)
The amount of money going into or out of the portfolio each month. Press the + / − button to switch between contributing (saving) and withdrawing (spending from savings). During retirement phases this is your household spending target before taxes.
💡 "Gross" means before tax. The Tax Profile card helps estimate how much more you'd actually need to withdraw to end up with your target after tax.
Add at (Start / End of period)
Whether the withdrawal or contribution happens at the beginning or end of each compounding period. "Start of period" is slightly more conservative for withdrawals.
One-Time Costs
Up to 5 large one-time expenses — like a renovation, a car, or a trip — each taken out of the portfolio at a specific month and year. Tap the header to expand, toggle each cost ON, enter the amount, and set when it happens.
💡 You can have multiple costs in the same year or spread across different years.
🏦 Gov't & Pension Income
QPP (combined)
Quebec Pension Plan — the government retirement pension both Keeron and Erin have contributed to through their working years. Combined amount for both. Kicks in at age 60 in the 60–65 phase. Currently $700/mo each = $1,400/mo combined.
💡 Because QPP reduces how much you need to take from your savings, it effectively extends how long your money lasts.
OAS (combined)
Old Age Security — a separate government benefit that starts at age 65. Combined for both Keeron and Erin, currently set to $1,400/mo total.
Pension (Erin)
Erin's defined benefit pension from her employer, paying $2,000/month starting at age 65. Guaranteed income that doesn't come from your savings at all.
💡 Together, QPP + OAS + Erin's pension cover $4,800/mo in retirement — meaning only $3,200/mo needs to come from your savings pool.
💛 Tax Profile — Keeron & Erin
Tax Modelling (ON / OFF)
Turns the tax estimate on or off. When ON, the calculator estimates how much federal and Quebec income tax you'd owe based on where your money is coming from. Only available during withdrawal phases.
TFSA %
What percentage of your total combined savings is in Tax-Free Savings Accounts. Money withdrawn from a TFSA is completely tax-free. Currently ~20% of your total portfolio (about $218,000 combined).
RRSP %
What percentage of your total savings is in RRSPs. Every dollar withdrawn from an RRSP counts as fully taxable income. Keeron has $171,000 and Erin has $340,000 in RRSPs, making up about 45% of the total.
💡 Best strategy: draw down RRSP early (55–60) while your income is lowest, before QPP and OAS push you into higher tax brackets.
Non-Reg % (auto)
The remaining percentage — Keeron's regular investment account ($393,000). Only the profit (gain) portion is taxable, and even then only half counts as income under Canada's capital gains rules.
NR Gain %
Of Keeron's non-registered investments, what percentage is profit vs. the original amount paid. Keeron originally invested $50,000 and it's now worth $393,000 — about 87% is gain. Only half of that gain gets added to taxable income.
Keeron Draw %
What share of each monthly withdrawal is attributed to Keeron vs Erin for tax purposes. Splitting income between two people almost always results in a lower combined tax bill than one person taking everything.
Draw Order
"RRSP First ★" draws from RRSPs before TFSAs — the smartest approach for most people in early retirement when income is lowest. Reduces the RRSP balance before mandatory withdrawals kick in at age 71.
📊 Results Card
Total Net Draw on Savings
The total amount taken out of (or added to) the portfolio over the entire phase, after income offsets like QPP, OAS, and pension.
Monthly Income Offset
The total government and pension income received each month. This reduces how much you need to take from your savings.
Net Monthly Draw on Savings
After subtracting income offsets, this is the actual amount coming out of your investment portfolio each month.
Ending Balance
How much money is left in your combined portfolio at the end of this phase. Automatically becomes the starting balance for the next phase.
Real Value (today's $)
The ending balance expressed in today's purchasing power. Tells you what your future balance is really worth in current dollars.
Est. Tax Owing / mo
Your share of combined federal and Quebec income tax for the month, based on your withdrawal amount and account mix (TFSA, RRSP, Non-Reg). This comes directly out of your monthly withdrawal — the portfolio balance is not affected.
After-Tax Monthly
What actually lands in your bank account after taxes are paid. This is your withdrawal minus the estimated tax owing — the real spending money available each month.
💡 Example: $10,000 withdrawal at 17.7% effective rate = $8,230/mo after tax.
Effective Tax Rate
The blended tax rate across your entire withdrawal — a weighted average across TFSA (0% tax), RRSP (fully taxable), and Non-Reg (capital gains at 50%) portions, split between Keeron and Erin.
🗂️ Life Phase Tabs
Now – 55
The current phase — Keeron and Erin are still working and contributing to savings. Duration: ~4 years. No government income yet.
55 – 60
Early retirement — both stop working. The full $10,000/month household draw comes from savings. No government income yet. This is the highest-pressure phase for the portfolio.
60 – 65
QPP kicks in for both ($700/mo each = $1,400/mo combined), reducing the net draw from savings. Household target is $9,000/mo.
65+
Full retirement — QPP + OAS + Erin's pension provide $4,800/mo in guaranteed income. Only $3,200/mo needs to come from savings. Duration: 35 years.
