❓ Help & Field Guide
🧮 Calculator — Main Fields
Principal
Read-only — automatically calculated as the sum of all Portfolio account balances. On Now–55 this is the sum of all five entered values. On later phases it carries forward from the previous phase's ending balance, scaled proportionally across all accounts.
Interest %
Expected average annual return. Typically higher in earlier phases (growth assets) and lower in retirement (conservative mix).
Inflation % & ADJ
Inflation defaults to 2.5% and ADJ is ON by default, meaning monthly withdrawals increase each year automatically to preserve purchasing power.
💡 ADJ ON is the more realistic projection — $9,000 today will need to be ~$10,200 in 5 years at 2.5% inflation to buy the same things.
Compounding
How often interest is applied. Monthly is the most realistic for typical investment accounts.
Duration (Years)
Length of the current phase. Now–55 is 5 years (based on Erin's birthday, Sept 17 1976). Bridge phases are 5 years each. Retirement defaults to 35 years.
Gross Monthly (+ or −)
Monthly cash flow in or out. Positive = contributing. Negative = withdrawing. During retirement this is gross spending before tax — see After-Tax Monthly in Results for what actually lands in the account.
↺ Reset
Restores the current phase's rate, inflation, compounding, duration, and contribution amounts from built-in presets. Does not change the principal or portfolio balances, which are always carried forward from real data.
🏦 Gov't & Pension Income
Availability
Faded and locked on Now–55 and 55–60 (no government income yet). Active at 60–65 when QPP begins, and fully loaded at 65+ when OAS and Erin's pension also start.
QPP (combined)
Quebec Pension Plan — combined monthly amount for both Keeron and Erin, starting at 60. ~$1,400/mo combined. Directly reduces how much savings need to be drawn.
OAS (combined)
Old Age Security — starts at 65. ~$1,400/mo combined.
Pension (Erin)
Erin's defined benefit employer pension, $2,000/mo from 65. Does not come from savings.
💡 At 65+, QPP + OAS + pension = ~$4,800/mo guaranteed, meaning only ~$3,200/mo needs to come from the portfolio.
💼 Portfolio — Keeron & Erin
Purpose
Enter the current dollar balance in each investment account. The Principal in the Calculator card auto-sums from all five fields and is not editable directly — the Portfolio is the single source of truth.
Now–55 vs Later Phases
On Now–55 all five fields are fully editable. On withdrawal tabs, Keeron's Non-Reg auto-derives as the remainder of Principal minus all TFSA and RRSP balances. The totals always add up correctly.
Carry-Forward
Switching to a later phase automatically grows each account at the current rate and scales proportionally to match the actual ending balance. On withdrawal phases, account balances deplete based on the selected draw order, and those depleted balances carry into the next phase.
Keeron — TFSA / RRSP / Non-Reg
Three account types. TFSA: completely tax-free on withdrawal. RRSP: every dollar withdrawn is fully taxable income. Non-Reg: only 50% of the capital gain portion is taxable (inclusion rate). Only Keeron holds Non-Reg investments.
Erin — TFSA / RRSP
Erin holds TFSA and RRSP only. Her RRSP is significantly larger (~$340K vs Keeron's ~$171K), which affects the tax calculation — see Tax Details for the per-person breakdown.
NR Gain %
Percentage of Keeron's Non-Reg balance that is profit (capital gain vs. original cost). Only 50% of the gain counts as taxable income.
📊 Results Card
Ending Balance + Real Value
The large green number is the nominal ending balance. Directly below it in grey italics is the real value — what that future number is worth in today's dollars at the current inflation rate. They live together so the context is always visible.
After-Tax Monthly
Always visible on withdrawal phases. The estimated amount that actually reaches the bank account after taxes. The single most important number for retirement planning.
Tax Details ▾ & Draw Order
Tap "Tax Details" to expand the full breakdown. The Draw Order dropdown in that same header controls which accounts get drawn first — change it and results update instantly.
Balanced 50/50 ★ (default)
Most tax-efficient strategy. Each person targets half the annual draw independently. Keeron draws from RRSP → Non-Reg → TFSA. Erin draws from RRSP → TFSA. Incomes stay equalized, preventing either person from being pushed into higher tax brackets unnecessarily.
💡 Once Keeron's RRSP depletes, he automatically shifts to Non-Reg (only 50% taxable) while Erin continues from her RRSP — a natural, tax-efficient progression.
RRSP First
Drains the combined RRSP pool (split by each person's balance share) before touching Non-Reg or TFSA. Aggressive de-risking of the most tax-expensive account type.
TFSA First
Drains tax-free accounts first — generally the least efficient strategy for tax purposes but useful for modelling scenarios where RRSP deferral is prioritized.
Keeron / Erin — Taxable & Tax
Year-1 taxable income and average annual tax per person. Canada taxes individuals, not couples — no automatic income splitting applies until RRIF pension splitting at age 65. The imbalance in RRSP sizes is a real cost in the 55–65 phases.
🗂️ Life Phase Tabs
Now – 55
Contribution phase — saving and growing. Enter all current account balances in the Portfolio card. Duration is 5 years (to Erin's 55th birthday, Sept 2031). No tax on withdrawals since none are made.
55 – 60
First withdrawal phase — full household draw from savings, no government income yet. Highest pressure on the portfolio. Balanced 50/50 draw order recommended here to equalize incomes before RRIF conversion.
60 – 65
QPP begins (~$1,400/mo combined), reducing net draw from savings. Both people are still in pre-RRIF territory — no pension income splitting available yet.
65+
Full retirement. QPP + OAS + Erin's pension = ~$4,800/mo guaranteed. At this point RRSPs convert to RRIFs and up to 50% of RRIF income can be pension-split between spouses (T1032), helping equalize taxes. Modelled over 35 years.
📋 Other Controls
Ledger
Year-by-year table of additions, interest, and opening/ending balances. Real (inflation-adjusted) values shown in the ledger rows where applicable.
One-Time Costs
Up to 5 large one-time expenses (renovations, vehicles, travel) each toggled ON with a specific month and year within the current phase.
📊 Chart
Visual balance projection — nominal and inflation-adjusted real value — for any phase.
📄 Export PDF
Full printed report covering all four phases: charts, summary tables, tax breakdown, and year-by-year ledgers.
