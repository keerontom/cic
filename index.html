❓ Help & Field Guide
🧮 Calculator — Main Fields
Principal
The total amount of money you're starting with — your combined savings pot at the beginning of each life phase. For the first phase (Now–55), this is all of Keeron and Erin's savings added together.
💡 When you move from one phase to the next, this number is automatically carried forward from the previous ending balance.
Interest %
The average annual return you expect your investments to earn. For example, 7% means your money grows by roughly 7% per year before withdrawals.
💡 Higher in early phases (more growth assets), lower in retirement (more conservative mix).
Inflation % & ADJ toggle
Inflation is how much prices rise each year — set to 3% here as a default. When the ADJ toggle is ON, your monthly withdrawals automatically increase each year to keep up with inflation (so your lifestyle is protected). When OFF, withdrawals stay the same dollar amount throughout.
💡 Turning ADJ ON is more realistic — $9,000 today won't feel like $9,000 in 10 years.
Compounding
How often your interest is calculated and added to your balance. Monthly is the most common and most accurate for most investment accounts.
Duration (Years)
How many years this phase lasts. The phases are pre-set: Now–55 is 4 years, 55–60 and 60–65 are 5 years each, and retirement (65+) is 35 years.
Gross Monthly (+ or −)
The amount of money going into or out of the portfolio each month. Press the + / − button to switch between contributing (saving) and withdrawing (spending from savings). During retirement phases this is your household spending target before taxes.
💡 "Gross" means before tax. The Tax Profile card helps estimate how much more you'd actually need to withdraw to end up with your target amount after tax.
Add at (Start / End of period)
Whether the withdrawal or contribution happens at the beginning or end of each compounding period. "Start of period" is slightly more conservative for withdrawals (money leaves before earning interest that period).
One-Time Cost
An optional one-time large expense — like a renovation, a car, or a trip — that comes out of the portfolio at a specific point in time. Toggle it ON, enter the amount, and set the month and year it happens.
🏦 Gov't & Pension Income
QPP (combined)
Quebec Pension Plan — the government retirement pension both Keeron and Erin have contributed to through their working years. This section shows the combined amount for both. It kicks in at age 60 in the 60–65 phase. Currently set to $700/mo each = $1,400/mo combined.
💡 Because QPP reduces how much you need to take from your savings, it effectively extends how long your money lasts.
OAS (combined)
Old Age Security — a separate government benefit that starts at age 65. Combined for both Keeron and Erin, currently set to $1,400/mo total.
Pension (Erin)
Erin's defined benefit pension from her employer, which pays out $2,000/month starting at age 65. This is guaranteed income that doesn't come from your savings at all.
💡 Together, QPP + OAS + Erin's pension cover $4,800/mo in retirement — meaning only $3,200/mo needs to come from your savings pool.
💛 Tax Profile — Keeron & Erin
Tax Modelling (ON / OFF)
Turns the tax estimate on or off. When ON, the calculator estimates how much federal and Quebec income tax you'd owe based on where your money is coming from. Only available during withdrawal phases.
TFSA %
What percentage of your total combined savings is in Tax-Free Savings Accounts. Money withdrawn from a TFSA is completely tax-free — it doesn't count as income at all. Currently ~20% of your total portfolio.
💡 Erin and Keeron each have contributed the maximum since TFSAs began — about $109,000 each.
RRSP %
What percentage of your total savings is in Registered Retirement Savings Plans. Every dollar withdrawn from an RRSP counts as fully taxable income — like a salary. Keeron has $171,000 and Erin has $340,000 in RRSPs, making up about 45% of the total.
💡 Best strategy: draw down RRSP early (55–60) while your income is lowest, before QPP and OAS push you into higher tax brackets.
Non-Reg % (auto)
The remaining percentage — money in regular (non-registered) investment accounts. Only the profit (gain) portion is taxable, and even then only half of it counts as income under Canada's capital gains rules. This is Keeron's $393,000 account.
NR Gain %
Of Keeron's non-registered investments, what percentage is profit (unrealized gain) vs. the original amount paid. Keeron originally invested $50,000 and it's now worth $393,000 — so about 87% is gain. Only half of that gain (43.5% of the withdrawal) gets added to taxable income.
Keeron Draw %
What share of each monthly withdrawal is attributed to Keeron vs Erin for tax purposes. Set to 50% by default, meaning each person is considered to withdraw half. This matters because Canada taxes each person individually — splitting income between two people almost always results in a lower combined tax bill than one person taking everything.
Draw Order
The recommended order in which to draw from accounts. "RRSP First ★" means drawing from RRSPs before TFSAs — this is the smartest approach for most people in early retirement when income is lowest. "TFSA First" saves RRSPs for last. "Proportional" draws from all accounts evenly.
💡 RRSP First is recommended because it reduces the RRSP balance before mandatory withdrawals kick in at age 71, preventing a large future income spike.
📊 Results Card
Total Net Draw on Savings
The total amount taken out of (or added to) the portfolio over the entire phase, after accounting for income offsets like QPP, OAS, and pension.
Monthly Income Offset
The total government and pension income received each month. This reduces how much you need to take from your savings.
Net Monthly Draw on Savings
After subtracting income offsets, this is the actual amount coming out of your investment portfolio each month.
Ending Balance
How much money is left in your combined portfolio at the end of this phase. This automatically becomes the starting balance for the next phase.
Real Value (today's $)
The ending balance expressed in today's purchasing power. Because of inflation, $1,000,000 in 20 years won't buy what $1,000,000 buys today. This number tells you what your future balance is really worth in current dollars.
Est. Household Tax / yr
An estimate of how much combined federal and Quebec income tax Keeron and Erin would owe in a given year, based on the withdrawal amounts and account types. Uses 2024 tax brackets.
Eff. Rate · Monthly Reserve
The effective (blended) tax rate on your withdrawals, and how much you'd need to set aside each month to cover the tax bill.
Gross Draw Needed to Net Target
If you want to actually spend your target amount (e.g. $9,000/mo), you need to withdraw more than that to cover taxes. This is the grossed-up amount you'd need to take out of your portfolio to end up with your spending target after tax.
💡 Example: if your effective tax rate is 18%, and you want $9,000 to spend, you'd need to withdraw about $10,976 to net $9,000 after tax.
🗂️ Life Phase Tabs
Now – 55
The current phase — Keeron and Erin are still working and contributing to savings. Duration: ~4 years (until Keeron turns 55 in January 2031). No government income yet.
55 – 60
Early retirement — both stop working. The full $10,000/month household draw comes from savings. No government income yet. This is the highest-pressure phase for the portfolio.
60 – 65
QPP kicks in for both Keeron and Erin ($700/mo each = $1,400/mo combined), reducing the net draw from savings. Household target is $9,000/mo.
65+
Full retirement — QPP + OAS + Erin's pension provide $4,800/mo in guaranteed income. Only $3,200/mo needs to come from savings to meet the $8,000/mo household target. Duration: 35 years.
📋 Ledger Tab
Year-by-year breakdown
Shows every year of the current phase: how much was added or withdrawn, how much interest was earned, and what the balance was at the start and end of each year. Real values (in today's dollars) appear below each year's ending balance when inflation is set.
⚙️ Defaults Tab
Save Defaults / Apply to Calc
Each life phase has its own saved defaults. Use "Save Defaults" to lock in your preferred settings for the current phase, and "Apply to Calc" to restore them if you've been experimenting with different numbers.
